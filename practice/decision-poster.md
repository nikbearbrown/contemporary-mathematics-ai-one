# Capstone: post the case for your event

A fictional community committee needs one public decision poster and one private calculation sheet. Use the cases from all five chapters. Complete the first pass without a model or the worked answer key. Your job is to tell people what they can plan around, and tell the committee where the facts could change.

## The brief

- **Meals.** Plan for 87 named reservations at 240 mL each, a 1,000 mL reserve, and whole 1.5 L trays. Separate total remainder from the amount available to walk-ins. Suppose the serving area is a rectangle 6 m by 4 m, of which a nonoverlapping 6 m by 1.5 m strip must stay clear for circulation. If each service station needs 3 square meters of usable space, give the area-based *upper bound* on stations; explain why it does not prove they physically fit.
- **Route.** Assume the lift has closed and the passage is unverified. Recommend a route for a guest who requests a step-free trip. Put a time estimate on it and identify an assumption to check.
- **Room programming.** Five slots are now available for the 5:3:1 requests. Show the allocation under unmodified largest remainder and under the stated one-slot minimum. Pick one policy and document its tradeoff. For opening activity, use the complete ballots and state the decision rule separately from the winner.
- **Money.** Use the $100 loss limit. Decide between the shelter options, then recompute after an $80 wet-state guarantee if it is verified. Distinguish a forecast from the hard constraint and state what you do not know.

## What to submit

1. A one-page poster: your earliest supportable promise about the route, the planned meal capacity, the allocation and opening activity, and a named contingency for one decision. Make all critical information available in text, without relying on color alone.
2. A calculation sheet showing units, eligibility and counts, the route edges used, the allocation and voting arithmetic, the cash flows by state, and at least one inequality or changed assumption. Name the two groups of people considered for meal service; do not count a person twice merely because the records overlap.
3. A short change defense. An assistant says, “The map shows an eight-minute passage and the outdoor event earns $47.50, so promise eight minutes and spend the expected earnings.” State what is correct in that quote, what is unsupported, and what evidence would change the recommendation. Check the two claims separately.
4. One fresh unaided check: defend your decision if the organizers grow the reserve to 1,200 mL and the one-minute passage is confirmed open and step-free. Identify what changes and what stays fixed.

## Self-check or reviewer rubric

Score each area 0 (missing), 1 (plausible without a reproducible trace), or 2 (correct, traceable, and properly qualified):

- Unit conversion, integer capacity and duplicate-aware counts.
- Directed, feasible route and distinction between estimate and promise.
- Explicit allocation rule and voting rule, with reproducible arithmetic.
- State-specific cash, expected value and downside against the constraint.
- Independent changed-input check and an explanation anyone can follow.

A good poster may lead to different decisions if it sets out different permitted priorities. What cannot change is the calculation for the stated assumptions. Model use after the first pass is optional; if used, include the fictional inputs and one independently checked correction.
