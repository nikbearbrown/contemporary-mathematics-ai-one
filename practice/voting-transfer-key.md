# Voting transfer key

Use this after attempting Chapter 4. The examples are fictional.

1. The edited ballot is still B-first, so the first count remains A 5, B 3, C 4. After B leaves, one ballot goes to A and two to C. The final count is 6–6; nothing exhausted. The supplied event rule does not decide the tie. Apply a tie provision announced beforehand, or acknowledge that a complete policy is missing.
2. R leads the first count 6–5–4 over S and T. T leaves, and all four T-first voters ranked R next, so R wins the instant runoff 10–5. T beats R 9–6 when S is ignored and S 10–5 when R is ignored. R's plurality lead alone establishes neither result. Give one point each for identifying the counting rule, tracing the tally or transfers, and making a correction supported by the marked ballots.
3. More than one policy can be justified. Check that the answer pairs its rule with its stated purpose, handles ties and incomplete ballots, and acknowledges one limitation. A claim of majority needs both the numerator and the population counted.
